The brief

Sell a village before it exists — by showing the life that comes with it.

Northville is not 100 houses. It's a community wrapped around a single round wooden building — gym, pool, preschool, restaurant, coworking, hotel, neighbors — and every part of it is reservable in one app. That app, not the house, is what we're really selling.

This sprint produces the first ambitious cut of brand assets: a 15-second hero film, three 6-second social clips with three different voices, twelve photographic visuals, and a strategy that turns the proposition into a charter — "Be one of the first 100 neighbors."

Strategy

Five moves that turn a real-estate project into a movement.

01 — The app is the ad

Every clip ends on the phone. The product feature ("Reserve pool", "Drop off preschool", "Guest hotel room") IS the marketing line. Showing the booking action is more persuasive than explaining the community.

02 — "The First 100" charter

Scarcity reframed as belonging. You're not buyer #4327. You're neighbor #17. Limits the audience, deepens commitment, generates word-of-mouth, builds a real waiting list before launch.

03 — The round Village Center is the logo

Apple has Apple Park. Northville has the round wooden Center with the copper roof. Every film, every still, every ad returns to it. It becomes ownable Nordic iconography.

04 — Persona, not target group

One spokesperson can't sell 100 homes. Three voices in this sprint (warm mom-type, calm professional, founder/architect) cover three different buyer reasons. Future cuts add young family, downsizing couple, remote-worker single.

05 — Built for relationships

The cultural counter-position: most housing is sold on square meters and yield. Northville is sold on the simple, uncommon promise that you'll know your neighbors' names. That line shows up in every script.

06 — Tone mix, one feel

Cinematic for the hero (long film, sajt-hero, YouTube). UGC handheld for social (Reels, TikTok, IG stories). Founder-portrait for credibility (LinkedIn, PR). Three modes. One palette: wood, light, calm.

The hero film — two versions, 15 seconds, 16:9

The campaign in one breath — and the same idea in two registers.

Same 15-second slot, same village, two very different tones. Version A is a long-form UGC endorsement — a neighbor speaking about why she's moving in. Version B is a cinematic Apple-style architectural film — no spoken words until the final tagline, just observed life across dawn, the round Center, and a twilight aerial.

Version A · UGC long-form endorsement · 15s
"Okay, Northville is unreal — 100 smart wooden homes circling a round Village Center… It feels like real community. I'm joining. Northville. A village built for relationships. Join the waiting list."
Use: LinkedIn organic, sajt-detalj, retargeting, founders sharing on personal accounts. Feels real, builds trust, low polish.
Version B · Cinematic Apple-style hero · 15s
"Northville. A village built for relationships. Join the waiting list."
Use: site hero, YouTube preroll (skippable 15), Meta brand awareness, sales-deck opener, cinema/event reveal. Aspirational, ARRI-look, no talking head.

Note: Version A was generated in Marketing Studio's default UGC mode (it auto-reframed the cinematic prompt as a creator testimonial). Version B was re-rendered in Wild Card mode with explicit "no talking head" direction to land the original Apple-style intent. Both kept — they serve different funnels.

The first 100 — who they are

Four people. Four very different reasons to want this. One village.

Persona 01

The Returning Family

34 + 36, two kids under 8. Tired of Stockholm prices and 90-minute round-trip preschool runs. Wants daycare 200 m from the kitchen and neighbors who babysit.

Buys for: preschool inside the Village Center.

Persona 02

The Downsizing Couple

57 + 59, kids grown. Sold the 280 m² villa, doesn't want a stale apartment in town. Wants design they can be proud of and a restaurant five minutes away.

Buys for: restaurant + design pedigree.

Persona 03

The Remote-Work Single

32, works for a Berlin startup. Hates working alone at home. Wants the coworking space as their "real" office and the pool as their lunch break.

Buys for: coworking + community.

Persona 04

The Founding Believer

Any age. Cares less about the house than about being one of the first 100. Wants the story, the charter, the feeling that they helped build something.

Buys for: belonging.

Beyond this sprint

Eight things to commission next — in priority order.

01

App demo film (8–12s)

A finger taps the app: "Reserve pool", "Drop off preschool", "Guest hotel room". The product is the ad. This is probably the single highest-converting asset we don't yet have.

02

Architect-in-the-place founder doc (60–90s)

Henrik walks through the round Village Center at golden hour and explains the philosophy in his own words. Drops on LinkedIn, opens every press conversation, justifies a premium price.

03

"A day in Northville" lifestyle film (30s)

One family, morning to evening: coffee → preschool drop-off → coworking → pool with kids → dinner with neighbors. Shows the WHOLE proposition without explaining it. The film you can run for two years.

04

Persona-expansion clips (3 × 6s)

Young family, downsizing couple, remote-work single. Same script structure as Clips A–C, different reasons, different voices. Triples the addressable Reels audience.

05

Master plan diagram + interactive map

An axonometric drawing of all 100 plots around the Center, with each plot clickable. Doubles as scarcity ("plot #23 is gone") and as a sales tool.

06

"Charter 100" landing experience

Not a waitlist form. A claim-your-place experience: name, why you want to be a neighbor, the founder reads every entry. Creates the highest-intent leads possible.

07

Sound brand

One ten-second acoustic motif (Scandinavian piano + soft pad) that ends every film. Free recognition once the rotation starts. Cheap to commission; compounding for years.

08

Local-market story arc

Bring the people of Västerås and Tillberga into the narrative: existing café gets espresso machine in the Center, local baker bakes the bread, school cooperates with preschool. Pre-empts NIMBY, deepens community story, generates earned media.
