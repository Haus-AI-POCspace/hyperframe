Djäkneberget — coffee & the city view

Walk · ~15 min uphill

Start at the central station and stroll up to the city's best lookout. Djäkneberget is a hilltop park with more than 400 names carved into the rock — an 1800s tradition where every prominent citizen was invited to chisel their name into the cliff. Grab a coffee at the little kiosk and let the legs wake up.

★ Tip From the lookout you can spot Mälaren — on a clear day the view runs all the way to the old castle at Strömsholm.

Turbinhuset — the industrial soul of Västerås

VOI scooter down to the harbour · ~7 min

Hop on an electric scooter and glide down to the waterfront. The Turbinhuset from 1891 is where Västerås went from a sleepy medieval town to an industrial powerhouse — and where ASEA (later ABB) was born. Press play below and let a voice tell you the whole story.

Turbinhuset & the story of Västerås

Press play to start

Welcome to the Turbine House.

You are standing where the river Svartån meets the bay, in the heart of Västerås. The year is 1891. For a thousand years, the waters of the Mälar valley have driven mills and hammers — but this little brick building is about to change everything.

The Turbinhuset was built to turn flowing water into electricity, making Västerås one of the first fully electrified cities in the world. From here, current flowed out to the ASEA workshops, to the houses, to the street lamps.

ASEA — the General Swedish Electric Company — grew up right here in the 1890s under engineers Jonas Wenström and Sigfrid Edström. A century later it would merge to become ABB, one of the largest industrial groups on Earth. It all began in this small brick building beside the water.

Before electricity, Västerås was a quiet medieval town with a cathedral, a harbour, and an iron-weighing station. But the power of the water transformed it into Sweden's Manchester — and the Mälar valley into the country's technological heart.

Take a moment. Listen to the water. It is the same current that turned the wheels that spring of 1891, when the future arrived in Västerås.

★ Tip Headphones on, walk slowly around the building while the voice plays — the riverside loop is exactly two minutes.

Västerås Art Museum — Karlsgatan 2

VOI · ~4 min

Karlsgatan 2 is a cultural house with both the Art Museum and the County Museum under the same roof. The focus is on contemporary Nordic art — a perfect palate-cleanser between industry and lunch. Free entry.

★ Tip Chat with one of the guards — they always have a favourite piece this week and love being asked.

13:00 — 14:30 1h 30min

Lunch at Pråmen

Walk through the harbour · ~10 min

Pråmen means "the barge" — and that's exactly what it is: a moored boat-restaurant at Lögarängen pier. Grab a deck table if the weather plays along, order the fish of the day or a classic Swedish shrimp sandwich, and watch the sun glitter on Lake Mälaren. The definition of a quiet lunch.

★ Tip Skip the reservation — arrive 15 minutes early and grab the first free deck table.
